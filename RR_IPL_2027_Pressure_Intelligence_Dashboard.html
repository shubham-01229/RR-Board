How pressure is identified

Transparent, reproducible rules applied at delivery level.

Chase pressure

In the final 5 overs of a chase (30 balls or fewer), a delivery is flagged if required run rate is at least 9.5, or 30+ runs are still required.

First-innings pressure

Overs 16–20 are flagged when the batting side has lost at least 4 wickets. This captures late-innings scoring with reduced wickets in hand.

Fair comparison

Pressure performance is compared with each player's non-pressure performance. Minimum samples: 60 pressure + 60 baseline batting balls; 48 pressure + 48 baseline bowling balls.

Scoring methodology

A comparative index, not a prediction of future performance or a measure of player reputation.

Batting score

50% strike-rate ratio + 30% survival-rate ratio + 20% boundary-rate ratio. Component ratios are capped between 0.5× and 1.5× to limit extreme small-denominator effects.

Bowling score

45% economy improvement ratio + 35% wicket-rate ratio + 20% dot-ball-rate ratio. Component ratios are capped between 0.5× and 1.5×.

Reliability adjustment

Scores are shrunk toward 100 according to pressure sample size: batting uses pressure balls ÷ (pressure balls + 60); bowling uses pressure balls ÷ (pressure balls + 48).

Interpretation & limitations: A score above 100 indicates better performance in flagged pressure situations than in the player's non-pressure baseline. The pressure definition is one defensible model, not the only possible one.
